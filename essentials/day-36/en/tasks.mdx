# Day 36. Tasks

<Subtitle>Lessons 2 and 3, 90 minutes. Warm-up — 20 minutes, main tasks — 50 minutes, starred tasks — 20 minutes. Today the tasks are arranged differently than usual: the warm-up is preparing your own defense, the main tasks are the defenses themselves and work with the feedback, the starred ones are for those who have defended and are waiting. If the group has many people and the defenses take all 90 minutes, the starred tasks become homework. We open everything through Live Server.</Subtitle>

## Warm-up

<Task title="1. One sentence" level="easy" time="6">
<Goal>name your project so that a stranger understands whether they need it.</Goal>

Write a sentence about your project in the three parts of section 2: what it is, what it does, how it
helps. Read it to a neighbor and ask them to say in their own words who this project is for.

<Hint>
If the sentence has the words “function”, “array”, `fetch`, it is a sentence about technology.
Ask yourself: what will a person be able to do with my project?
</Hint>

<Criteria>
- the sentence is said in half a minute and has no words about technology;
- the neighbor named in their own words who the project is for, and got it right;
- if they got it wrong — the sentence is rewritten, and the second time they named it correctly.
</Criteria>
</Task>

<Task title="2. The scenario and the initial state" level="easy" time="7">
<Goal>prepare the demo so that you do not spend time on small things at the defense.</Goal>

Write the demo scenario as a table “action — what you see”, as in section 12 of day 35.
Prepare the phone by the table “What to prepare in advance” from section 3 of the lecture and bring
the project into its initial state.

<Hint>
The initial state — with your own reset button, or by removing the key and reloading the page
right away. On the computer, for the questions, open the same network address as on
the phone if you want to see the same data.
</Hint>

<Criteria>
- the scenario has from five to eight rows, each one an action and what you see after it;
- the scenario is gone through on a phone from start to end without stops;
- after that the project is in its initial state again;
- it is written down at what address the project is open on the phone and on the computer.
</Criteria>
</Task>

<Task title="3. A decision and a mistake" level="easy" time="7">
<Goal>prepare two minutes of the defense that show you thought it through and did not guess.</Goal>

Choose one decision and one mistake of your project. Write the decision out in the four
parts of section 4, the mistake — in the five steps of section 5.

<Hint>
For “what would be different” the best is something that can be shown in half a minute,
like the saved deck in section 4. For the fifth step of the mistake, a check function
like `testInput` will do.
</Hint>

<Criteria>
- the decision has all four parts, and “what would be different” is checked, not made up;
- the mistake has all five steps, the fifth is a check that is silent now;
- both stories are read out loud with a clock: each one is no longer than a minute.
</Criteria>
</Task>

## Main tasks

<Task title="4. Your own defense" level="medium" time="12">
<Goal>defend the project in seven minutes: the sentence, the demo, the decision, the mistake, questions.</Goal>

Present in the order of section 1. The demo is on a phone, by the scenario of task 2.
Answer questions with the moves of section 7: a place in the code, a check in the console, a reason or
an honest “not checked — into ‘later’”.

<Hint>
If something breaks during the demo, read the red line out loud and ask the data.
This is not a failure but one more demo.
</Hint>

<Criteria>
- the defense fit into seven minutes, and the questions had their own time;
- the demo was on a phone, every action with what changed on the screen;
- not a single answer is made up: either code, or the console, or “I will check”;
- all questions and remarks are written down — they are needed in task 7.
</Criteria>
</Task>

<Task title="5. The reviewer" level="medium" time="13">
<Goal>ask questions that help to show the project, and give feedback that can be acted on.</Goal>

You are the reviewer of the neighbor whose project you read on day 35. Before their defense, prepare three
questions — one of each kind: “where”, “what if”, “why”. Ask the first question,
and after the defense give the feedback of two parts from section 8.

<Hint>
The best first question is about a weak spot from your remarks of day 35: what became of it?
</Hint>

<Criteria>
- three questions are written down before the defense, each one specific, each one can be answered with code or a check;
- not a single question is about the person or “why not like mine”;
- the feedback has one strong side with a place in the code and one suggestion that can be checked;
- if a question got the answer “I will check”, you checked it together after the defense.
</Criteria>
</Task>

<Task title="6. The timekeeper and the listener" level="medium" time="12">
<Goal>keep the time of someone else’s defense and listen so that you have something to ask.</Goal>

At one of the defenses be the timekeeper: start the timer from section 1 of the lecture in
the console when the presenter begins, and raise your hand at every border. At
the other defenses be a listener: for each one write down one question.

<Criteria>
- the timer was started on time, its messages matched the raised hand;
- it is written down which part of the defense went over its time and by how much;
- for every defense you listened to there is one written question, and at least one of them was asked.
</Criteria>
</Task>

<Task title="7. After the defense" level="medium" time="13">
<Goal>turn the questions and feedback of the defense into a plan and finish the project with an honest retrospective.</Goal>

For every question and remark from your defense write down an answer: do it, explain or
postpone. Do one “do it” right away. Then
write a retrospective of the four questions of section 12 and compute the plan and the actual time of the whole
project with code.

<Hint>
If your project has shuffling or sorting, check that it does not stand before
building buttons or lists from the same data: reload the page a few times —
nothing must jump.
</Hint>

<Criteria>
- every remark of the defense has an answer, and every “postpone” has a row in “later” with minutes;
- one edit is made and checked the same way it was found;
- the check functions are silent after the edit;
- the retrospective answers each of the four questions, and the plan and the actual time are computed with `reduce`.
</Criteria>
</Task>

## Starred tasks

<Task title="8. A timer with a warning" level="hard" time="10">
<Goal>extend the program from the lecture so that it warns in advance.</Goal>

The timer of section 1 only reports the start of the parts. Extend it: 30 seconds before
the end of every part that lasts longer than half a minute — the message
“30 seconds left: …” with the name of the part.

<Hint>
The end of a part is its start from `schedule` plus its `minutes`. In
`forEach` the second parameter is the number of the element, and with it you can take
`defense[i].minutes`. Not to wait seven minutes, move
`60 * 1000` into a constant `MINUTE` and for the check set
`MINUTE = 1000`: the whole defense will pass in seven seconds.
</Hint>

<Criteria>
- the warnings come at 3:00, 4:00, 5:00 and 6:30 — four of them;
- the sentence, which lasts exactly half a minute, has no warning;
- the messages about the start of the parts and “time is up” stayed as they were;
- checked with `MINUTE = 1000`: the warnings come at seconds 3, 4, 5 and 6.5, “time is up” at 7.
</Criteria>
</Task>

<Task title="9. Space flips the card" level="hard" time="10">
<Goal>do the first task from “later” and find a trap that is not visible on the first press.</Goal>

Add to the “Cards” a `keydown` listener on `document` (day 27):
Space flips the card like a click. Check two cases: the focus on the page and the focus
on the “I know” button — after it was clicked with the mouse.

<Hint>
In the second case look at the counter and `learned`. A focused button
is pressed by Space by itself — who besides your listener heard this key? Remember
`preventDefault` of day 27.
</Hint>

<Criteria>
- Space with the focus on the page flips the card, one more Space brings the word back;
- it is written down what Space does without the fix when the focus is on “I know”: how many words became learned;
- after the fix, Space with the focus on any button only flips the card;
- on a learned topic Space gives no red lines.
</Criteria>
</Task>
